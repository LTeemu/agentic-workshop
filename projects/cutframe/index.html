Film · 2026 · 4 min

Night Drive

One motorway, one cassette, one headlight out. A short about the space between exits — and the people who never take them.

Still 48:12 — the exit that never came.

The road south of the city is a long grey ribbon the film never lets us see end. We sit in the passenger seat of a '92 sedan while the driver talks to a radio station that stopped broadcasting in 2019. Every cut is a passing streetlight; every song change is a small death.

We cut the picture to the beat of a single demo tape, then recut it against the hum of the engine itself. The final version keeps both — the music bleeding into the motor, the motor answering the music — until you can't tell which one is driving.

“Nobody remembers the drive. Everybody remembers the song when it ended.”

Night Drive premiered at a drive-in, obviously. The projector threw the road across three screens while the audience sat in their own cars, engines idling, headlights low. Nobody honked. That felt like applause.

Music video · 2025 · 3 min

Pulse / Noise

A band recorded in a boiler room. We made the edit dance with the pipes.

Still 01:03 — the pipes keep the time.

Some music videos try to look expensive. This one tries to sound loud. Shot on a single afternoon in a working boiler room, the band plays facing the machines, and the machines play back — valves hissing on the off-beat, steam timing the choruses.

The edit is 130 cuts in three minutes, which sounds frantic until you realise the source footage was one locked-off camera. Every cut is a jump inside the frame: from a drum skin to a pressure gauge, from the singer's mouth to the red-hot door of the furnace.

“When the music drops out, the boiler keeps the beat.”

We finished the grade at 3 a.m. and played the film back on a phone at full volume. The neighbours complained. The band called it the best review they'd ever had.

Brand film · 2025 · 2 min

Meridian

A compass rose for a company that builds observatories. North is the client; the sun is the schedule.

Still 00:58 — noon, arriving for the first of many times.

When the brief arrived it said, in full, "time, but for our brand". Meridian is the answer: a two-minute film about noon — the moment the sun crosses the line, shadows fold, and everything is briefly, perfectly aligned.

We shot one location, four seasons, and a single gold hour across twelve months of waiting. The edit stitches the years together so noon arrives once and keeps arriving — each cut a new shadow pointing the same way.

“Every shadow is a sundial if you wait long enough.”

The client's legal team asked if the sun was trademarked. We told them we'd check with the copywriter. The film shipped with the sun uncredited, as requested.

Short · 2024 · 5 min

Static Bloom

A flower that only opens at the frequency of a dead channel. Horror, but soft.

Still 03:31 — the hiss between channels.

Television static has a sound — sixty hertz of snow. Static Bloom is a five-minute film about a flower that opens only when that sound plays, and a woman who keeps the last working set in the city just to watch it. Once a night, for as long as the signal holds.

We built the flower from paper and wire, then let the edit tear it apart and rebuild it: every petal a cut, every cut a frame of noise. The horror isn't loud. It's the hiss between channels.

“The flower doesn't know the channel is dead. It just knows the frequency.”

It screened at one festival, won a jury mention, and a reviewer wrote that the ending "felt like holding a dial tone". We keep that review pinned above the timeline.

Music video · 2026 · 2 min

Chorus Line

A choir with no conductor, a mix with no guide track. The edit keeps the beat by hand.

Still 01:47 — the choir holds its breath.

The brief said "make us sound alive". The band had recorded the chorus 240 times, once per take number, and wanted to use all of them. We said no. We used six — and cut the rest into the shape of a single held note.

Every line in the mix is a different take of the same word. The edit weaves them so the word repeats like a pulse — one singer inhaling while another exhales, the room breathing as one machine.

“The best take is the one you don't hear.”

We graded it in the dark with the volume at eight, and when the last frame landed on the beat, one of us applauded. The other was the whole audience. It still counts.

Short · 2023 · 8 min

Last Print

A photo lab on its final night develops one last roll for a customer who never picks it up.

Still 06:12 — the paper comes up blank.

The lab had one night left. The owner, who had printed twelve thousand weddings, was developing a single roll for a regular who hadn't shown up in three months. The red light went on. We shot the darkroom with one camera and no lights at all.

The ninety-minute wait cuts to eight, and every pause stays: the timer, the tray, the tongs, the moment the paper slides into the developer and turns from white to almost-white. It's a film about looking — at a photograph the way you'd watch a horizon.

“Nothing develops if you keep looking away.”

The customer never came. The owner framed the blank print and hung it above the till, where the register used to be. The last thing the lab ever sold was the picture of nothing that was left.

Brand film · 2025 · 1 min

Reel to Reel

Sixty seconds for a tape company that still believes in the thing on the reel.

Still 00:38 — the tape is nearly out.

The client makes mastering tape, so the material is the product — the film could have toured the whole factory or the whole philosophy. We chose the machine: one reel, the tape between two spools, and the sound of the deck breathing.

The edit is two shots. Reel A to reel B in a single matching cut at the exact moment a bass note lands, and then the whole film playing in reverse until the tape is wound back to the start. Sixty seconds, one idea, no B-roll.

“The cut is the splice. Everything else is just tape.”

The client asked for a version in colour. We told them our colour was room temperature and the deadline was the reel. They approved the mono cut; the factory tour went to a rival.

Short · 2026 · 6 min

The Courier

A bike messenger races the deadline across a city that doesn't know it's in the film.

Still 02:19 — the light turns just for him.

The track follows one courier from dawn to the drop — a route the city drew for him and forgot. We shot from the handlebars, the curbs, and one very patient drone. The edit is the map: every turn a cut, every cut a street he'll never ride again.

We cut to the sound of the derailleur and the wind, and layered the city underneath — sirens, shutters, a market waking up. The deadline is real: the script was delivered to the actor's basket after the final ride, sealed with wax.

“A city is just a route with people in it.”

The drop happens at frame 8640 exactly. We counted. The courier didn't — he was already two blocks south, taking the long way home because the film was done.

Film · 2024 · 11 min

Tide Line

One lighthouse, one keeper, one storm that arrives exactly on time.

Still 05:44 — the beam finds the water.

The keeper had kept the light for forty years and the film wanted to know why. Tide Line is the answer, told in eleven minutes and one season: the sea in, the sea out, the beam turning, and the keeper turning the lamp because the motor was sentimental.

We mounted the camera on the lantern rail and let the rotation do the editing — every revolution a lap of the clock. The storm arrives at minute nine; we cut its arrival to the second the keeper finally looks out, not at the sea, but along the beam.

“The light doesn't warn the ships. It keeps them company.”

The keeper retired a week after the premiere. He watched the film twice, said the storm took longer in real life, and left the key in the door. Somebody else has the light now. It turns the same.
